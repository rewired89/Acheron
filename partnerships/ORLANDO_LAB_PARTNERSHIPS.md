   `https://nanoscience.ucf.edu/hickman/about/`
   Not a genetic-circuit lab — but arguably a **better** fit for Acheron
   than one would be. This lab does exactly the bioelectric/microelectrode

   live cell voltage, iPSC-derived neurons patterned onto electrodes,

   "read the voltage" half of the project, from a lab that already has

   systems work.

2. **NanoBioSensors & Systems Laboratory** — Prof. Swaminathan Rajaraman,

   `https://nanoscience.ucf.edu/rajaraman/group/`
   Also not genetic-circuit work — focus is micro/nanofabrication of

3. **Burnett School's Molecular Microbiology division** (19 faculty) was
   checked and is genuinely the closest UCF department-level match to
   "genetic engineering of organisms" — but its stated focus is microbial

**What this means practically:** for the genetic-circuit-engineering half

## Partnership Models (How to Get Lab Access)

### Model 1: Undergraduate Research Collaboration (BEST for students)

**How it works:**
- You're not joining the lab full-time
- You're proposing a specific project (Experiment A)
- You provide: Your labor, reagents budget ($2K), computational analysis
- They provide: Bench space, equipment access, cell culture training

**Pitch template:**

```
Subject: Interdisciplinary Research Proposal - Bioelectric Computing

Dear Dr. [Name],

I'm a cybersecurity student working on a novel bioelectric memory
architecture inspired by morphogenetic bioelectricity (Levin lab).
I've completed extensive computational modeling and am ready for
experimental validation.

PROJECT: Test if arbitrary voltage patterns persist 24+ hours in
gap junction-coupled cell networks (HEK293 + Connexin-43).

WHAT I BRING:
- Computational expertise (image analysis, error correction algorithms)
- $2-3K budget for reagents (plasmids, dyes, consumables)
- 10-20 hours/week commitment for 4-6 months
- Novel interdisciplinary angle (potential for NSF funding)

WHAT I NEED:
- Access to fluorescence microscope + cell culture facility
- Mentorship on transfection and imaging protocols
- Co-authorship on publication

This sits at the intersection of synthetic biology and unconventional
computing - a growing field with strong funding opportunities. I believe
your expertise in [their specialty] would be invaluable for understanding
[specific mechanism].

Would you be open to a brief meeting to discuss?

Best regards,
[Your name]
UCF Cybersecurity Student
```

**Success factors:**
- Keep it short (1 page max)
- Be specific about what you need
- Emphasize what YOU bring (not just asking for favors)
- Mention funding potential (labs care about this)

### Model 2: Independent Study / Directed Research Course

**How it works:**
- Register for independent study credits (UCF likely offers this)
- Find biology faculty willing to supervise
- Do your experiment as course project
- Get academic credit + lab access

**Advantages:**
- Formalized relationship (not just informal)
- You're an official student in their lab
- Might get access to teaching microscopes, shared equipment

**How to set up:**
- Talk to your department (cybersecurity/CS) about cross-department independent study
- Approach biology faculty with course proposal
- Work out: What are deliverables (paper, presentation?)

---

### Model 3: Pay for Core Facility Access

**How it works:**
- Many universities have "core facilities" that charge per use
- Example: UCF NSTC might have hourly microscope rates
- You pay, you get access (no need for lab partnership)

**Costs:**
- Microscopy: $50-$150/hour (expensive but possible)
- Cell culture space: $100-$300/month (bench rental)

**Advantages:**
- Don't need to find PI sponsor
- More flexibility in scheduling
- Professional relationship (customer, not student)

**Disadvantages:**
- More expensive ($1K-$2K just for equipment access)
- No mentorship on protocols
- Might still need help with cell culture training

**Where to check:**
- UCF NSTC website - look for "user fees" or "core services"
- Email core facility manager, ask about external user rates

---

### Model 4: Online Mentorship + Local Lab Services

**How it works:**
- Find remote mentor (e.g., contact Levin lab, explain project)
- They guide you on experimental design
- You hire local lab services company to run experiments

**Lab services companies:**
- **Biocytogen (offers custom cell experiments)**
- **CROs (Contract Research Organizations)** - search "cell biology CRO Florida"
- **Lab Services companies** - they run experiments for pay

**Costs:**
- Much higher ($5K-$10K for full experiment)
- But: Professional results, no need for training

**When to use:**
- If you have funding but not time
- If you can't find local lab partner
- If you want guaranteed results

## The "Secret Weapon" Approach

### Partner with a Biology Student (Mutual Benefit)

**How it works:**
- Find UCF biology undergrad or Master's student
- They need research projects for their degree
- You provide: Idea, computational expertise, project direction
- They provide: Lab access (through their advisor), hands-on work

**Where to find them:**
- UCF biology department bulletin boards
- Student research symposiums
- Biology club meetings
- Post on UCF research forums

**Pitch to biology student:**
```
"I'm a cybersecurity student with a funded research project in
bioelectric computing. I need a biology collaborator who can handle
cell culture and microscopy. You'd get:

- First-author publication (or co-first author)
- Unique project (bioelectricity + computing = hot field)
- Computational collaboration (I handle image analysis, modeling)
- Potential for graduate school application boost

Looking for someone with:
- HEK293 cell culture experience
- Access to lab through existing advisor relationship
- Interest in synthetic biology or bioengineering

This could be your undergraduate thesis project."
```

**Advantages:**
- They already have lab access (through their advisor)
- Win-win collaboration (both get publication)
- Peer-to-peer is often easier than student-to-PI

### Reframe the Narrative:

| ❌ What They Fear | ✅ How to Address |
|------------------|------------------|
| "You don't know cell culture" | "I'm willing to learn. Can you recommend training resources or could a lab member mentor me on protocols?" |
| "This isn't biology" | "This is synthetic biology - engineering living systems. My computational background is essential for the analysis." |

| "This is too ambitious" | "I'm starting with the simplest possible test: one pattern, HEK293 cells, 48-hour timescale. The scope is well-defined." |
