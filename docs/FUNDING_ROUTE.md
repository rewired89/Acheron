## The one fact the decision depends on

SBIR/STTR awards go to a for-profit small business that is **more than 50 percent
owned and controlled by US citizens or permanent residents** (13 CFR 121.702, as
recalled), and the principal investigator must normally be primarily employed by
that company at award. Whether the founder (Dayana Sanchez) is a US citizen or
permanent resident is **not recorded anywhere in this repository**, and it decides
the structure:

| If the founder is a US citizen or permanent resident | Either founder can hold any share; SBIR eligibility is straightforward |
|---|---|
| If she is not | The co-founder (Jacqueline Solano, a US citizen) would have to hold more than 50 percent of the company for SBIR eligibility. That conflicts with the founder's wish to keep authority, so the alternatives below matter |

Do not guess this; it is the founder's to answer, and an immigration or
corporate attorney should review any structure built around it.

## Decision (recommended order, cheapest and most reversible first)

1. **Now, no money from anyone:** (a) answer the patent attorney's two questions
   (`patents/PATENT_GAP_ANALYSIS_2026.md`) and ask whether to file a provisional on
   draft A before any outside sharing; (b) send the protocol to a
   microbiologist (`docs/MICROBIOLOGIST_OUTREACH.md`).
2. **Pilot P0 on small money:** Pilot P0 builds one strain and runs a calibration;
   it is the cheapest honest start. Options, cheapest first: a university core
   facility fee-for-service or a collaborator's bench, a paid synthetic biology
   contractor, rented bench space. The earlier DIY-lab idea is realistic only for
   cloning and plate work; flow cytometry usually needs a core facility.
3. **Non-dilutive, once ownership is settled:** NSF SBIR/STTR (start with the NSF
   Project Pitch, a short pre-application) for a company formed to hold the IP.
   SBIR takes no equity and no share of profits; the government receives limited
   use rights under the award (verify exact terms with an attorney). It is slow
   (months) and does not fund before a company exists.
4. **University partnership only on written terms** that keep inventorship and IP
   with the company (sponsored research agreement with the company owning
   inventions made by its staff, or a service agreement for a core facility).
   Co-authorship is negotiable; ownership is what to protect. Have an attorney
   read any such agreement before signing.
5. **Crowdfunding drafts** (`docs/pitch/EXPERIMENT_COM_CAMPAIGN.md`, Reg CF
   draft) stay drafts; the Reg CF one needs a securities attorney. Do not launch
   either before step 1 is done, because a public campaign is a public disclosure.
