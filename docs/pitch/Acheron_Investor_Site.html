01 · The problem

Every biocomputing product today runs on neurons, cells built for speed, not for storage.

Neurons need continuous power to hold any signal at all, run on a fixed replacement clock, and need life support: temperature, pH, oxygen control. None of that survives radiation, vacuum, or physical damage, exactly the conditions spacecraft, seafloor sensors, and remote infrastructure operate in.

02 · The hypothesis

Some living tissue already holds a voltage pattern for weeks, and regrows itself after damage.

01

Natural precedent

Planarian flatworms store body-plan identity, "grow a head here", as a bioelectric pattern. Cut one in half, the pattern survives and rebuilds correctly in both halves.

02

Our question

Can the same kind of system, voltage state plus strong regenerative inheritance, hold arbitrary binary data, not just the organism's own natural signal?

03

Why it would matter

A substrate that needs no power to hold state, and repairs itself after physical damage, is a fundamentally different answer to extreme-environment storage.

Write. The organism regenerates or divides. The state travels with it, not just genetic material, the written data itself. The original colony heals back to a clean baseline, ready to write again.

The full write → transfer → self-heal cycle. Not yet tested in a wet lab, it's the goal the roadmap experiment works toward.

03 · Foundation & method

Built on published results, tested against our own model, not just believed.

Three independently published results this concept is built on, plus the modeling approach used to test them.

LEVIN ET AL., 2006–2023 (TUFTS)

Bioelectric body-plan control

Voltage gradients control regeneration outcomes in planaria, replicated across multiple labs. The two-headed-worm result is the most cited example.

GARDNER, CANTOR & COLLINS, 2000, NATURE

Engineered genetic bistability

A synthetic two-repressor circuit holds one of two states indefinitely once flipped, a solved, 25-year-old result the authors called an addressable cellular memory unit.

AKABUOGU ET AL., 2024, NANO LETTERS

Non-destructive electrical readout

A simple two-electrode impedance measurement distinguishes live from dead bacterial biofilm, tied to one ion channel's activity. No dye, no sample destruction.

Modeling approach: physics-based, BETSE-inspired simulation (gap-junction diffusion, ion-channel membrane dynamics), the same framework underlying the Levin Lab's own BETSE tool, cross-validated against tasmorpho (Blattner, 2026, bioRxiv), an independently published BETSE-based method whose reference values we reproduce to six decimal places.

04 · What we found

We stress-tested our own claims before anyone outside the project did.

Acheron's credibility strategy is leading with rigor, not hype. Here is what held up, what broke, and what we changed as a result.

FAILED UNDER NOISE

Passive persistence isn't enough on its own

Our first model showed a planarian cell could passively hold a written bit for 24+ hours. Under realistic biological noise, that same result collapses from 100% to about 50%, a coin flip. We found this by stress-testing our own model, not from an outside reviewer.

Fidelity at 24h
100% → ~50%

Zero-noise simulation vs. realistic channel, metabolic, and thermal noise added.

HOLDS ACROSS THE CATALOG

The engineered fix works across its whole practical design range

The pivot: an actively engineered bistable circuit (Gardner topology, built from real Bacillus BioBrick Box parts). Any promoter pair within the BBB's entire characterized 1.0x–10.0x strength range holds state at 100% fidelity at 24 hours. Only pushing far outside that catalog, to a severe 0.1x mismatch, collapses the circuit to a monostable dead switch.

Genetic & voltage-output fidelity
100%

Across the entire characterized promoter-strength range; 50% only at a deliberate, far-out-of-catalog 0.1x mismatch.

NEW RISK, FOUND EARLY

The colony's own biology can scramble a reading, even when the data is fine

B. subtilis biofilms run a continuous potassium-wave electrical signal of their own (Prindle et al., 2015), unrelated to anything Acheron builds. In simulation, that native oscillation can corrupt a single instantaneous voltage reading even when the underlying circuit data is 100% intact.

The fix, and its limit
~3h

Reading via impedance (EIS) instead of raw voltage helps, but a single fast sweep isn't immune either, it needs to integrate across a full oscillation cycle to recover accuracy.

Cross-organism honesty check

We ranked our own candidate mechanisms by how much wet-lab risk each still hides.

B. subtilis, engineered circuit HIGHEST CONFIDENCE Inheritance is mechanistically forced: cell division copies the existing protein state. This is now the project's lead mechanism.
B. subtilis, methylation MEDIUM CONFIDENCE Real fidelity numbers exist in the literature, but measured in a different species. The fallback if the engineered circuit doesn't survive regrowth.
Planarian, bioelectric circuit LOWEST CONFIDENCE Our original organism choice. Cell-coupling strength is a borrowed assumption that has never been measured. We're reporting that now, before spending wet-lab money on it.
