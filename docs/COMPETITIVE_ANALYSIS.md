> four neuron/organoid players. See `docs/SCIENTIFIC_AUDIT_2026.md` Part 10
> for full detail:
>
> 1. **Mycelium memristor** (LaRocco et al. 2025, *PLOS ONE*), a real,
>    published self-repairing fungal memristor, 90±1% accuracy up to 5.85
>    kHz, survives dehydration/rehydration. Closest published self-healing
>    bioelectric device to date, but it's an analog resistance element,
>    not an addressable binary memory with a write/read software layer, and
>    90% accuracy would fail this project's own ≥70%-integrity-AND-≥3:1-SNR
>    bar for a memory system.
> 2. **DNA-sequence data storage in *B. subtilis*** (Yachie & Tomita 2007)
>   , arbitrary data (120 bits) already stored in this exact organism's
>    genome and read back via sequencing. Confirms the organism can hold
>    arbitrary data at all, but as an archival, destructively-read DNA
>    sequence, a fundamentally different engineering problem than a
>    dynamic, non-destructively-readable physiological/bioelectric state.
> 3. **BioCompute Inc.** (founded 2024), a funded startup (WTF Fund, Grad
>    Capital, 1517 Fund) commercializing exactly that DNA-sequence-storage
>    category at scale, with a real product roadmap and capital behind it.
>    This is the closest thing to a funded, direct commercial competitor
>    found so far, worth naming explicitly in any pitch, not omitting.
>
> None of the three use a dynamic bioelectric/physiological state as the
> data-bearing signal, which remains this project's actual differentiator
>, but "nobody else is doing bioelectric memory at all" (the original
> framing below) is no longer accurate; "nobody else is doing *dynamic,
> rewritable* bioelectric memory" is the current, defensible claim.

## Executive Summary

The biocomputing market is nascent but rapidly commercializing. All major players use **neurons** as their computing substrate. This creates five critical gaps that Acheron can exploit by using **planarian somatic cells** instead.

## The Five Critical Gaps

### Gap 1: Volatile vs. Persistent Memory
**Problem:** Neurons = volatile RAM
**Our Solution:** Planarian somatic cells = persistent "bio-hard drive"

Neurons must maintain active signaling to hold state. Planarian bioelectric gradients are stable morphogenetic attractors that persist without energy input.

### Gap 2: Cell Death vs. Regeneration
**Problem:** All neurons die (6 months - 2 years)
**Our Solution:** Planarians regenerate indefinitely

Cortical Labs' most expensive operational cost is replacing neurons every 6 months. Our chips self-heal.

### Gap 3: Fragility vs. Extreme Environment Survival
**Problem:** Neurons require strict lab conditions
**Our Solution:** Planarians survive radiation, temperature extremes, vacuum

**Target Markets:**
- Space hardware (radiation-resistant)
- Deep-sea systems (pressure, cold)
- Military/sovereign computing (EMP resistance)

### Gap 4: No Bioelectric Write/Read Interface
**Problem:** Everyone grows cells, no one has encoding software
**Our Solution:** Binary ↔ bioelectric pattern translator

**This is the licensing opportunity:**
- Sell to Cortical Labs, Koniku, FinalSpark
- Platform-agnostic (works with neurons OR planarians)
- Revenue stream before hardware ships

### Gap 5: High Operational Costs
**Problem:** Life support systems, controlled environments
**Our Solution:** Hardy, low-maintenance planarian cultures

Cortical Labs CL-1 requires continuous life support. Planarians tolerate wide environmental ranges.

## Conclusion

**No company is using planarian somatic cells for bioelectric computing.**

The closest competitor (Morphoceuticals) uses Levin's work for medicine, not computing. The biocomputing companies (Cortical Labs, Koniku, FinalSpark) all use fragile, short-lived neurons.

**Acheron's wedge:**
1. Prove pattern persistence in simulation (3 months)
2. Open-source the encoding software (build moat through community)
3. Partner with academic lab for wetware (6 months)
4. Target space/extreme environment contracts (differentiated market)

**The bioelectric write/read interface is the immediate revenue opportunity** - it's substrate-agnostic and can be sold before we ship hardware.
